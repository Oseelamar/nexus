01 / WHY WE EXIST

Progress means understanding what we build.

Artificial intelligence is advancing rapidly. But capability alone is not enough. To build systems we can rely on, we need to understand how they learn, how they reason, and how they behave when the world does not match our expectations.

NEXUS studies the foundations of machine intelligence with a focus on making AI systems more understandable, robust, and aligned with human intentions.

We believe meaningful progress requires more than building increasingly capable systems. It requires the scientific tools to investigate them, the discipline to evaluate their limits, and the openness to question what we think we know.

02 / RESEARCH

Four perspectives. One underlying question.

We investigate the mechanisms behind intelligent systems and the conditions required to make them reliable. Our work spans the foundations of learning, the evaluation of model behavior, and the challenge of understanding what happens inside increasingly capable systems.

  1. 01Robustness / Evaluation / Reliability

    AI Safety & Reliability

    Understanding when AI systems fail, why they fail, and how to make their behavior more dependable. We investigate robustness, evaluation, uncertainty, and the gap between performance in controlled settings and behavior in the real world.

  2. 02Learning / Representations / Generalization

    Foundations of Machine Intelligence

    Studying the mechanisms that allow models to learn, represent information, reason, and generalize. We seek a deeper account of the capabilities that emerge during training and the principles that govern them.

  3. 03Interpretability / Alignment / Model Behavior

    Alignment & Interpretability

    Investigating how a model’s internal processes relate to its observable behavior and intended objectives. We explore ways to make model behavior more legible and to better understand where its actions may diverge from human intentions.

  4. 04Reproducibility / Transparency / Measurement

    Open Science & Evaluation

    Building a stronger foundation for shared progress through transparent methods, reproducible experiments, careful measurement, and the open exchange of research. Scientific claims should be open to scrutiny, not insulated from it.

03 / THE QUESTIONS

The difficult questions are the important ones.

Some of the most consequential questions in AI remain unresolved. We approach them as scientific problems: questions to investigate, hypotheses to test, and assumptions to challenge.

  1. Q—01

    Can we predict failure before it happens?

    How can we identify the conditions under which a model becomes unreliable, especially when it encounters situations that differ from its training and evaluation environments?

  2. Q—02

    What is happening inside a model?

    To what extent can we understand the representations and computational processes that give rise to a model’s behavior, rather than relying only on its outputs?

  3. Q—03

    What does reliable evaluation actually measure?

    How can we distinguish genuine capability from superficial performance, and design evaluations that remain meaningful as models become more capable?

  4. Q—04

    How do we maintain alignment as capabilities grow?

    What evidence would demonstrate that a system’s behavior remains consistent with its intended objectives across unfamiliar situations, changing contexts, and increasingly complex tasks?

04 / METHOD

Rigorous by design. Open to revision.

Good research is not a straight line from hypothesis to conclusion. It is a process of investigation, experimentation, evaluation, and revision.

Our approach is built around making claims that can be examined, methods that can be repeated, and findings that can withstand scrutiny.

Revision — any stage can send the work back

  1. 01

    Investigate

    Define the question. Examine prior work, identify assumptions, and develop a clear account of what remains unknown.

  2. 02

    Experiment

    Translate questions into testable hypotheses. Design controlled experiments and investigate the mechanisms behind observed behavior.

  3. 03

    Evaluate

    Measure results carefully. Examine failure cases, test alternative explanations, and distinguish strong evidence from suggestive results.

  4. 04

    Share

    Communicate methods, limitations, and findings clearly so that others can examine the work, reproduce results, and build on what has been learned.

A working framework, not a fixed sequence. Most projects move back and forth between stages.

    06 / PRINCIPLES

    What matters is not only what we build, but how we know.

    i. Scientific integrity
    Claims should follow evidence. We value careful measurement, intellectual honesty, and the willingness to revise our conclusions when the evidence demands it.
    ii. Safety as a research priority
    Capability is not the only measure of progress. Understanding risks, limitations, and failure modes is fundamental to developing AI responsibly.
    iii. Openness to scrutiny
    Research is stronger when methods and assumptions can be examined. We value transparency, reproducibility, and constructive criticism.
    iv. Human-centered progress
    AI should be developed with serious consideration for the people and societies affected by its behavior. Technical progress must remain connected to human intentions and consequences.
